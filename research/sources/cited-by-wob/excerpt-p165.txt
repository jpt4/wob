Bertrand Russell, Principles of Social Reconstruction
London: George Allen & Unwin, 1916
Internet Archive: https://archive.org/details/cu31924032577532
(Cornell University Library scan. Printed page 165 is PDF page 169.)

Cited by W. O. Brigstocke, 11 September 1917 (047697), as the
passage from p. 165 used to illustrate "form". The phrase he
quotes, "destructive and indifferent to authority", is on this
page. The sentence breaks at the foot of p. 165 and the
paragraph ends at the top of p. 166. That close — thought as
the chief glory of man, after the terror — is the cadence
Russell defends in his reply of 14 September 1917 (047709).

The "passage on Charity" in the same letter is not in this
book. Russell's reply treats it as St Paul's (1 Corinthians 13)
and says that Paul's reply cannot be obtained.

--- page 165 (Education), from "Men fear thought" ---

Men fear thought as they fear nothing else on earth—more than
ruin, more even than death. Thought is subversive and
revolutionary, destructive and terrible; thought is merciless
to privilege, established institutions, and comfortable habits;
thought is anarchic and lawless, indifferent to authority,
careless of the well-tried wisdom of the ages. Thought looks
into the pit of hell and is not afraid. It sees man, a feeble
speck, surrounded by unfathomable depths of silence;

--- page 166, completing the paragraph ---

yet it bears itself proudly, as unmoved as if it were lord of
the universe. Thought is great and swift and free, the light
of the world, and the chief glory of man.
