W. O. Brigstocke to Bertrand Russell
Rugby, 11 September 1917
Autograph letter, two sides of a folded sheet. Bertrand Russell Archives, RA1 710, box 5.05.

A pencil "11" stands on the blank first panel and is not part of the letter. The reconstruction Brigstocke says he encloses is not in this file.

Rugby.
11. ix. 17.

Dear Sir—

You were kind enough to answer me before: I trust you will pardon my bothering you again.

I am writing a book for use in schools on the writing & teaching of English. I had intended to include a passage from "Principles of Social Reconstruction" (p 165.) which I have used to illustrate "form". We have played with the passage & pulled it to pieces and reconstructed it. My excuse must be that to get boys to think one must be "destructive and indifferent to authority." I enclose one of our reconstructions: I should like to be allowed to add it by the side of the original: and if I may do so, it would help me if you could kindly say in what way our reconstruction fails to express your meaning. We have studied your passage with the passage on "Charity" which we tried to reconstruct with lamentable result! In a weak moment I promised my boys to ask you to let us know what you think of our impudence.

Yours truly
W. O. Brigstocke
