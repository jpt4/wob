29 Shakespeare Road
Bedford. Sunday, 18. xii. 21

Dear Mr Russell,

I have just thought of a way of making my x, y, z business a little less vague. It is only an analogy, but it illustrates what I'm thinking of. I took x = the part, y = the part that is conceived, z as the context i.e. that part of x which is not y and by means of which y is 'seen'.

The analogy is this: Speech sounds are made of vowels & consonants: in vowels the sound (i.e. the audibility of it) is produced by air + vibration + [modification of] the shape of the opening: in other words it is vibration modified by pressure. In consonants we have air obstructed & every consonant can be made with or without vibration (voiced or unvoiced).

If we call the 'thing' y a vowel kind of thing, and z a consonant kind of thing, and when z is some data it is 'voiced' & when it is odds it is 'unvoiced', there is enough analogy I think to show the kind of thing I am groping after.

You may remember that you told me I was wrong in supposing that there was no difference between maths & language. Wittgenstein maintains that 'one' is language, & that 'two' isn't. I don't follow him. The best I can do at present is to say that I am now sure you are right & that the nearest I can get to any statement of the difference is this: The mathematical one is conspicuous in the absence of background; the logical one is conspicuous by its absence in a background. I add the one in the hope that it may throw some light on the vacuum business!

Yours sincerely
W. O. Brigstocke
