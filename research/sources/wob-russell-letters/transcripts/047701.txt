Typed transcription of the autograph letter 047700, two pages, with a carbon copy of the same transcription (scan pages 3–4). Bertrand Russell Archives, RA1 710, box 5.05.

Pencil on the ribbon copy: "1149." at upper left, and "Rej" at upper right. The carbon has neither mark. The typed signature reads "W. V. BRIGSTOCKE." Line-end hyphens are joined below. The carbon text is the same and is not repeated.

4 Hatherley Road
Kew Gardens.
7. v. 1919

Dear Sir—

Thank you for your letter : I have only just received my manuscript from the typewriter.

What I enclose is a preface and on reading it through again I see it wants rewriting : but you will be able to judge what I am driving at : I hope you will consent to read also chapter 1 when it is ready.

It is extraordinarily difficult to know how to write for any one who won't use symbols. When I bought your Introduction to Mathematical Philosophy the bookseller hinted that I had better keep my halfguinea, for, he said Look here and here — and he pointed to various tit bits he had discovered which all seemed to me quite common sense — why the fellows mad ! And I can assure you that that opinion is shared by at least one mathematical master at Rugby ! If the public is to be converted, they must be got to understand. And I'm sure they can't understand your last volume. I want to become theoretically sound : and for that purpose I am working very hard at languages : I can now read eight European ones and am learning a ninth : I am also making slow headway with Russian, Persian and Japanese. The last, especially is going to be of immense help : I knew I was going

to learn something as soon as I got a drift of the writing and understood that there is no article, that adjectives are conjugated and that a sentence is usually in the English order if you read it backwards. And I am preparing for understanding how and what the public understands by having accepted an offer from the Pelman Institute to join their staff of directors and write a new course for them. If ever I am in a position to give up all bread and butter work I shall devote three years to Mathematics : and then I shall have something to think about. In the meantime I hope you will make allowances for work produced by a wage earner with a family to feed at present prices !

Yours faithfully

W. V. BRIGSTOCKE.
