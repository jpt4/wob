W. O. Brigstocke to Bertrand Russell
Rugby, 16 September 1917
Autograph letter, two sides of a folded sheet. Bertrand Russell Archives, RA1 710, box 5.05.

Rugby.
16. ix. 17.

Dear Sir,

I am very grateful to you for your kindness in answering my question so fully.

What you say will be of great interest to several boys who objected strongly to the reconstruction but could not explain why.

What you say makes me think that I had a larger share in the work than I thought, for you say that the reconstruction expresses what is certainly not my view — that thought is so great that few men have the strength to be worthy of it and that it is a tyrannic master inspiring a few men to freedom but the majority to a terror which is their ruin if they do not run away from it. Our laughter at Mrs Shelley's: "Teach him to think for himself? Oh, my God! Teach him to think like other people." —

but can one deny that thought leads a man to a selfishness from which a mother may well pray to have a son delivered "if it be possible"?

You say at the end that you "are much honoured": I know that this is a conventional phrase but it gave me great pleasure. I often have occasion to speak of you to boys & colleagues. You will not be surprised to hear that they often show a remarkable ignorance of the kind of honour due to your name!

Yours truly
W. O. Brigstocke.

In "is so great", the word "great" is written over another word.
