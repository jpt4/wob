W. O. Brigstocke to Bertrand Russell
29 Shakespeare Road, Bedford, 17 December 1921
Autograph letter, two pages, written across the printed heading of the Royal Societies Club, which is struck through. Bertrand Russell Archives, RA1 710, box 5.05.

A pencil mark stands at the upper left. The printed heading, cancelled, reads: TELEGRAMS, "AQUILÆ, LONDON". TELEPHONE NOS REGENT 4024 & 4025. ROYAL SOCIETIES CLUB, ST. JAMES'S STREET, S.W.1.

29 Shakespeare Road
Bedford. 17. xii. 21

Dear Mr Russell,

Thank you for your very kind letter. My vocabulary of philosophy was widened on one of those experiences one does not forget! I had heard of your many adventures, but not of your son. Please tell him I admire his taste in fathers & that I hope he will continue as well as he has begun.

It is very kind of you to ask me to call: as I am here over Xmas I have attempted to write a kind of apology. I am not sure there is any connection between my philosophy & the economic divide. But you will probably understand what I mean when I say that in pessimistic moments I feel that philosophy is all bosh & that I ought to be doing honest work. But since I saw you I have received some encouragement: and I suppose it is not entirely due to mere politeness.

I believe a piece of practical work would teach me a lot: the fact that it was officially recognised would give me confidence and also enable me more easily to get access to the sources of information. And last but not least, the grant of a loan would give me courage to spend some money which in my present condition I do not feel justified in doing for what may be mere moonshine — if Schiller likes my M.S. And if you think I may do useful work, I am sure Wildon Carr would put in a word for me, & with an official blessing I might set to work under more reasonable conditions than at present without feeling that I was acting unfairly to my family.

With many thanks
Yours sincerely
W. O. Brigstocke.
