W. O. Brigstocke to Bertrand Russell
4 Hatherley Road, Kew Gardens, 27 April 1919
Autograph letter, one page. Bertrand Russell Archives, RA1 710, box 5.05.

4 Hatherley Road
Kew Gardens. 27. iv. 19.

Dear Sir—

Some weeks ago I suggested to Professor Muirhead that a translation of Vaihinger's "Philosophie des Als Ob" would be a useful addition to the "Library of Philosophy." He had not read it: but when he had seen my copy, he said he thought a re-statement would be better than a translation — and said he would consider any M.S. I offered to send him. I therefore set about it. But I am not at all sure that what I am writing has anything to do with Vaihinger: and if it hasn't it is probably valueless.

You will not remember, but some years ago you were good enough to help me & I did a good deal of reading with one of your pupils: I have also read your books carefully, and it puzzles me to see how quite clever people can ignore very important discoveries. Either I have mis-read you or the world is singularly blind and stupid: for if your statements are true, we have got to start rearranging all our ideas just as people had to when it was found that the earth was not the centre of the universe. The educational world, for instance, does ~~not~~ seem to think that it doesn't matter whether you are right or wrong!

Remembering your kindness in the past, I venture to ask whether you could spare the time to read the first few pages of my M.S.? If you could do that & tell me frankly what you think of it, then I should know whether I am fit to teach or whether I ought to be still learning.

Yours faithfully
W. O. Brigstocke
