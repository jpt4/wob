ROYAL SOCIETIES CLUB,
ST. JAMES'S STREET,
S.W.

7. xii. 21

Dear Mr Russell,

I write to ask whether you could support me in applying for a Government _loan_ for scientific investigation.

I am reading a paper on Probability to the Aristotelian Society which will be a summary of a book I hope to finish next Mar. I am hoping to leave off where Keyser & other mathematicians start. If mathematics starts with 1 and ends by finding what is = 0, I, as a linguist, claim to start with 0 and end up somehow with what is = 1. I have worked through various statistical results of science that more or less illustrate the processes of language as I conceive them. But I am inclined to think that an original piece of statistical work composed specially for the purpose would be more suitable as the practical illustration of my thesis. I suggest working out what does not at present exist (i.e. the 0) and show how it becomes 1; and I can think of no better field than North America where in a short time there will be a great _economic divide_, as Wade explained in his lecture last night (I enclose cutting).

I am probably going to Mauritius for a short time later in 1922 to do the work mentioned in the enclosed letter. Then I have six months or so free & I should like to spend ^the time^ considering the Great Divide.

As regards the money, what I really want is official recognition: I can't afford to risk so much on a private venture. But it seems to me a sound "proposition", for I am almost certain to be able to get help from the U.S.A. and the Canadian Government — in fact I shall have to, if I am to do it properly — it is quite possible that a profit may be made. But I am quite willing to sacrifice £500 if necessary _when I have done it_. The difference may seem small, but it makes all the difference to me, as the £500 securities I offer to hand over are all I have.

In case you have forgotten me, I enclose a couple of testimonials.

Yours sincerely
W. O. Brigstocke.
