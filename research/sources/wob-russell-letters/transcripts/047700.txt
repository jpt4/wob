Autograph letter, two pages. Bertrand Russell Archives, RA1 710, box 5.05.

A typed copy of this letter, with a carbon, is 047701. Where a word was hard to read, that copy has been used to settle it. The autograph abbreviations are kept here.

4 Hatherley Road
Kew Gardens. 7. v. 19

Dear Sir—

Thank you for your letter: I have only just received my M.S. from the typewriter.

What I enclose is a preface & on reading it through again I see it wants rewriting: but you will be able to judge what I am driving at: I hope you will consent to read also chapter 1 when it is ready.

It is extraordinarily difficult to know how to write for any one who won't use symbols. When I bought your Introduction to Mathematical Philosophy the bookseller hinted that I had better keep my halfguinea, for, he said Look here and here — and he pointed to various tit bits he had discovered which all seemed to me quite common sense — why the fellows mad! And I can assure you that that opinion is shared by at least one mathematical master at Rugby! If the public is to be converted, they must be got to understand. And I'm sure they can't understand your last volume. I want to become theoretically sound: and for that purpose I am working very hard at languages: I can now read eight European ones & am learning a ninth: I am also making slow headway with Russian, Persian & Japanese. The last, especially, is going to be of immense help: I knew I was going to learn something as soon as I got a drift of the writing & understood that there is no article, that adjectives are conjugated & that a sentence is usually in the English order if you read it backwards.

And I am preparing for understanding how & what the public understands by having accepted an offer from the Pelman Institute to join their staff of directors & write a new course for them. If ever I am in a position to give up all bread & butter work I shall devote three years to mathematics: and then I shall have something to think about. In the meantime I hope you will make allowances for work produced by a wage earner with a family to feed at present prices!

Yours faithfully
W. O. Brigstocke.
